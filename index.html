The Dollar Illusion

US Equities, Commodities and Bitcoin Re-Priced in Gold, 2000–2026

The S&P 500 rose 5.0× in dollars over 26 years. Measured in gold it fell about 67% from its 2000 peak. Re-priced in crude oil the same index ends 92% higher — so the sign of the conclusion depends on which hard asset you pick as the yardstick.

Was It Worth the Risk?

Volatility, Drawdown and Recovery in US Equities, Metals, Energy and Bitcoin

If gold out-compounded equities, the natural objection is that it simply paid for more risk. It didn't, on most measures: 17.9% volatility against the S&P's 19.2%, a milder worst drawdown, and roughly double the return per unit of volatility. Two measures cut the other way.

Conditional Risk

A Two-State Regime Model of Gold, US Equities and Oil, and What It Corrects in Part 2

Part 2's headline volatility number describes the wrong thing. Gold is more volatile than the S&P in calm conditions (13.3% vs 11.1%) and comparable when stressed. Its lower average arises only because it spends less time in the turbulent state.

Part 4

Error from assuming constant correlation

Regime-Dependent Correlation

Does Diversification Fail When It Matters, and Would It Change Anything?

Correlations do converge in crises — for oil, and for the Nasdaq, which was never a diversifier. For gold the correlation falls under stress. And the whole effect is swamped: rising volatilities add 11.47pp to crisis portfolio volatility, the correlation shift takes away 0.48.

Part 5

Observable but Not Anticipable

Leverage, Forced Liquidation and Cascade Clustering in Crypto Perpetual Futures

Crypto publishes the positioning data that regime models elsewhere must infer. It confers no predictive power: adding it to price features degrades out-of-sample AUC. One result survives — cascades cluster. Includes a report of two data-provenance errors made during the work.

Part 6

Bitcoin → S&P, n.s. across 88 episodes

Directional Contagion Between Volatility Regimes

Equities, Commodities and Bitcoin

When the S&P is turbulent and another market is calm, that market is dragged in — oil 2.27×, gold 2.15×, Bitcoin 1.21×. Nothing propagates back. The best-powered cell in the design is the one everyone assumes runs the other way, and it is null.

On negative results. Several parts in this series report findings that failed. Part 5's central hypothesis is a null, and the paper documents two data-provenance errors made during the work along with the procedures adopted to stop them recurring. They are published because a series that only reports what worked is not measuring anything.
